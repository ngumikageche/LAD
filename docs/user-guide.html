Read this first

Two different things decide what appears on your screen. Your role decides which menu entries exist for you at all; your scope decides whose records those screens then show. A screen that opens but looks empty is almost always the second one — see section 2, and Troubleshooting.

This guide describes what to do in the interface. Its companion, Working With Permissions, describes how access is granted in the first place, and is written for whoever administers roles.

Section 1

Getting started

Signing in

  1. Open LAD and sign in with your email address and password on the Welcome Back screen. Accounts are created for you — there is no public self-registration.
  2. You land on the dashboard for your account type. There is no portal to choose: LAD reads it from your account and sends you to the right one.
  3. If a permission changes while you are signed in, sign out and back in. Permissions are read once, at sign-in.

The three portals

Learner

Your own record: marks, subjects, attendance, exams, portfolio evidence, and feedback on your trainers.

Lands on /student/dashboard

Trainer

The subjects assigned to you and the learners on them — attendance, marks, assessments, and class analytics.

Lands on /trainer-hub

Staff / Administrator

People, academic structure, and oversight across whatever part of the organisation you are responsible for.

Lands on /admin/dashboard

A staff account with neither a learner nor a trainer profile is treated as the third kind. If such a role has not been granted the admin dashboard, it lands on Reports instead, so there is always somewhere to arrive.

Finding your way

  • The sidebar is grouped by job — Overview, People, Academics, Reports, Attendance. Click a group heading to collapse it, and the arrows at the top to narrow the whole sidebar to icons.
  • The sidebar only lists what you may open. If an entry is not there, your role has not been granted it — it is not hidden behind a menu somewhere else.
  • Screens that build up over several stages label them Step 1, Step 2, and so on, top to bottom. Work down the page.
  • Tables share one set of controls: click a column heading to sort, use the search box to narrow, and page through at the bottom.
Section 2

What you can see

Every list, report, and dashboard in LAD is filtered to you before it is fetched. Four levels exist, narrowest first. You sit at exactly one.

Trainer The subjects assigned to you, and the learners, marks, attendance, and documents that belong to them.
Head of department Every course, trainer, and cohort in your department — across all the trainers in it.
College administrator Everything belonging to your college. Not the other colleges, even with a full administrative role.
Group super admin Every college. This is the only level that reads across the whole group.

A learner is narrower still: they see themselves, and nothing else, on every screen.

Why an administrator does not see every college

What separates a college administrator from the group super admin is the institution recorded on their account, not the strength of their role. An account attached to a college is held to that college; the group account is attached to none, which is what lets it read them all. Cross-college oversight for anyone else is granted deliberately, as a single visible setting, and never arrives as a side effect of some other permission.

Reports that are narrowed say so. The Enrolment & Attendance Overview and the Attendance Overview both print the scope they are showing — "Your assigned subjects only", your department, or your college — so an unexpectedly short report reads as your scope rather than as missing data.

Empty is not the same as broken

A newly created trainer sees nothing at all until subjects are assigned to them. Their permissions may be complete and the screens will still be empty, because a trainer's data comes from their assignments. Ask an administrator to assign your units.

Section 3

For learners

Check in to class

Attendance → Check In. Your trainer displays a QR code that changes every few seconds.

  1. Open Check In. Allow location access when your browser asks — LAD requests it while you are still lining up the code, because the code expires faster than a location fix usually arrives.
  2. Point your camera at the QR code. No camera? Choose Enter code manually and type the six-character session code your trainer reads out.
  3. Confirm the check-in. Your position is compared with your trainer's, and you must be inside the radius they set for the session.
If the check-in is refused

GPS failed means you were outside the allowed radius, or your location was too imprecise to tell — move nearer a window and try again. Not enrolled means you are not on that subject's roster. Duplicate means you are already marked present. Your trainer can record you manually in any of these cases.

See your marks

Academics → My Scores lists every recorded mark with its assessment, grade, and whether it passed. Reports → Report Card assembles the same marks into a printable termly card, and Reports → Practical Reports shows competency outcomes with the full task breakdown.

A mark is a percentage of the assessment it belongs to, so a paper out of 40 and one out of 100 are directly comparable. The pass threshold is 50%.

Your subjects, documents, and portfolio

  • My Subjects — what you are enrolled on, with your average in each.
  • Documents — material your trainers have shared for those subjects.
  • My Portfolio — upload evidence against the competencies your course requires, and track what is still outstanding.
  • Online Exams — exams your trainer has published, sat in the browser.

Attendance and standing

Reports → Attendance shows your present, absent, and late records for the period. Sustained attendance below 75%, or an average below 50%, raises an alert to you and to the trainers who teach you; it clears itself when the figures recover. Disciplinary Records lists anything formally recorded against you.

Feedback, both directions

Trainer Feedback is feedback written to you. Rate My Trainers is where you submit yours.

Section 4

For trainers

Your dashboard

The Teaching Performance Hub opens on all your assigned subjects. The Assigned subject scope selector at the top narrows every metric, chart, and panel below it to one subject — class average, mastery, attendance signal, at-risk learners, the competency heatmap, and the cohort comparison all follow it.

Take attendance

Attendance → Take Attendance runs a live QR session.

  1. Start Attendance Session, and pick the class. Allow location access — your position is the centre of the check-in radius.
  2. Set the duration (5 minutes is the recommended scan window), the allowed radius in metres, and how often the code refreshes.
  3. Display the QR code. It regenerates on the interval you chose, so a screenshot passed to an absent learner expires almost immediately. The six-character session code beneath it is the fallback for anyone without a camera.
  4. Watch check-ins arrive live, then end the session.

Attendance → Manual Attendance covers everyone the QR flow missed: select the class, then mark the register by hand.

Enrol learners onto a subject

People → Learner Enrollment. Choose one of your assigned subjects, then either enter a single learner's registration number or email, or import a roster. Template downloads a CSV with the columns the importer expects — registration_number and/or email. The import reports how many were enrolled, how many were already on the subject, and how many could not be found.

Enrolment attaches, it does not create

This screen puts existing learners onto your subject. A learner who has never been added to the institution has to be created first, on the Students screen or through Data Import.

Record marks

For a handful of marks, use the score form on your dashboard. For a whole class, use Academics → Bulk Upload Marks, which works down the page in four steps.

  1. Select Assessment. Pick the assessment the marks belong to, then Download Class List — an Excel file already filled in with your learners and the codes the importer needs. Its assessment code is shown with a Copy Code button.
  2. Select Subject (optional). Sets the subject for every row that does not name its own. Applies to the whole batch.
  3. Upload Marks File. Fill in the marks column of the downloaded class list and upload it. You may also attach scans or photographs of the original scripts as evidence. Then Preview & Validate.
  4. Review & Commit. The preview flags every invalid row, every learner who already has a mark for this assessment, and every learner the commit would attach to the subject. Nothing is written until you commit.
Always start from the downloaded class list

It carries the learner, assessment, module, and subject codes already filled in, so the only column you touch is the mark. A hand-built spreadsheet is where mismatched codes come from.

Assessments marked from three CATs have their own layout: choose the scenario and the class list carries a column per CAT, with the final mark computed for you.

Review and edit recorded marks

Academics → Score Management lists every mark in your scope. Filter to one subject with the subject dropdown, search by learner, course, subject, or assessment, and sort any column. Marks can be edited or removed here, and the filtered set exported to Excel, PDF, or CSV.

Track progress

Academics → Progress gives one row per learner with average, pass rate, assessment count, trend, and standing. The subject filter re-averages every learner over that subject alone rather than merely hiding rows, so the summary cards above the table describe the subject you picked. Needs attention first puts the weakest learners at the top.

Assessments and material

  • Practical Assess. — author practical assessments and record competency outcomes against them.
  • Online Exams — build an exam, publish it, and review submissions.
  • Documents — share material with the learners on your subjects.
  • Competencies — the competency framework your assessments evidence.

Your reports

  • Class Performance — averages, pass rates, ranking, grade distribution.
  • Syllabus Coverage — planned against covered topics.
  • Learner Attendance — the attendance record for your classes.
  • Trainer Reports — the exportable set of the above.

Feedback

Provide Feedback writes to a learner — the at-risk panel on your dashboard links straight into it with the learner and subject already selected. My Feedback is what learners have submitted about you.

Section 5

For administrators

People

  1. People → Users creates the account: name, email, role, and institution. The institution is what places the person in a college — without it they are not confined to one.
  2. People → Trainers then assigns teaching. Use Assign Subjects to pick course, module, and subjects. A trainer sees nothing until this is done.
  3. People → Students manages learner records and the course each is enrolled on.
  4. People → Roles defines what each role may open. It is covered in full by the companion permissions guide.
  5. People → Data Import brings people in in bulk rather than one at a time, and reports conflicts before anything is written.

Academic structure

The hierarchy runs Institution → Department → Course → Module → Subject, and each level has its own screen under Institution. Scope is resolved by walking this chain, so a course left without a department, or a module without a course, will drop cohorts out of reports. Terms defines the academic periods that reports group by; exactly one is active, and reports default to it.

Marks and oversight

  • Score Management — every mark in your scope, filterable by subject, editable, exportable.
  • Bulk Upload Marks — the same four-step flow trainers use.
  • Progress — learner-by-learner standing across your scope.
  • Attendance Report — sessions, check-in outcomes, and trainer activity for the trainers you oversee.
  • Compliance — the audit view of what has and has not been recorded.

Dashboard and analytics

Admin Dashboard is the headline view — enrolment, pass rates, term trend, department performance. Analytics is the same picture with the filters and breakdowns to interrogate it: by department, course, module, subject, trainer, or learner.

Taking access away

Removing a permission changes it for everyone holding that role. To narrow one person, move them to a narrower role instead. To stop a trainer seeing a class without touching their permissions at all, remove the subject assignment — that is what their visibility is built from.

Reports → All Reports is the catalogue of everything you can open, filtered to your role.

Getting a report out

  • Print uses a print stylesheet — filter controls drop away and the header becomes a titled, dated document.
  • Excel writes one sheet per breakdown.
  • PDF writes the same tables as a document.
  • CSV writes the main table only, for loading elsewhere.
Exports follow your filters

Every export carries the rows currently on screen, not the unfiltered set, and records who generated it and when. Set the filters first, then export.

Section 7

Troubleshooting

What you are seeingWhat it means
A menu entry is missing Your role has not been granted it. The sidebar, the address, and the underlying data all unlock together, so there is no other route to it.
"You do not have permission to view this" You reached a screen your role cannot open. Ask an administrator for the key that screen needs.
The screen opens but is empty The common case, and it is scope rather than permission. If you are a trainer, check your subject assignments; otherwise check the institution on your account.
A trainer sees no learners or marks No units are assigned to them. Trainers → Assign Subjects. Permissions are not the problem.
A report shows less than expected Check the scope label printed on it, and the term. Reports default to the active term, and marks filed under a different term will not appear.
A permission change had no effect Permissions load at sign-in. Sign out and back in.
Check-in refused — GPS failed You were outside the session's radius, or your position was too imprecise. Move nearer a window; the trainer can mark you manually.
A bulk upload row is rejected The preview names the reason per row. Almost always a code that does not match — start again from the downloaded class list rather than a hand-built sheet.
Marks uploaded but missing from a report They are probably filed under another term, or the learners are not attached to a course, so they cannot be grouped into a class. The Exam Results report says which.
A diagnostic order that works

Ask in this order: (1) Is the entry in my sidebar? (2) If it opens but is empty, whose records should it show — am I in scope? (3) If I am a trainer, do I hold the unit? (4) Is the report on the right term? Nearly every question resolves at one of those four.

Section 9

Glossary

TermMeaning in LAD
InstitutionA college. The top of the academic hierarchy, and the boundary most staff are held to.
DepartmentA division of a college, owning courses and trainers. The boundary a head of department is held to.
CourseThe programme a learner is enrolled on, at a CBET level.
ModuleA block of a course, containing subjects.
SubjectThe unit actually taught. Trainers are assigned subjects, and it is the assignment that decides what they see.
AssessmentA specific piece of marked work, with a total and a pass mark. Marks belong to assessments.
CATContinuous assessment test. Three of them can be combined into one final mark by a chosen formula.
CompetencyA skill a learner must evidence. Practical assessments and portfolio evidence are recorded against these.
TermThe academic period reports group by. One is active at a time, and reports default to it.
ScopeWhose records a screen shows you, decided separately from whether you may open it.
SessionOne live attendance sitting, with a rotating QR code, a time limit, and a radius.
At riskA learner averaging below 50%, or attending below 75% over the last 90 days.
